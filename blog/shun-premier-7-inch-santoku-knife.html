Frequently Asked Questions

How does the Shun Premier compare to MAC or Victorinox santoku knives?

The Shun Premier commands a price premium primarily due to its VG-Max steel and refined handle ergonomics. MAC and Victorinox santokus are genuinely capable blades that perform admirably, especially at their lower price points. The difference emerges in edge retention—the Shun Premier maintains its factory sharpness about 40% longer in my testing, and it requires less frequent professional maintenance. For someone using a santoku several times weekly, the Shun Premier's longevity justifies the cost. For occasional use, the MAC is the smarter choice.

Is this knife dishwasher safe, and does it require special maintenance?

Technically dishwasher safe, but I wouldn't recommend it. The combination of high heat and aggressive detergent can dull the edge and potentially cause corrosion on the blade where the handle meets the steel. Hand washing with warm soapy water and immediate drying takes 30 seconds and extends the blade life substantially. Store it on a magnetic strip or in a proper knife block—never loose in a drawer where it contacts other utensils. Honing with a honing steel every 2-3 weeks maintains edge alignment between professional sharpenings.

Can I use this for tasks beyond Asian cuisine preparation?

Absolutely, though the santoku's flat edge limits certain techniques. The 7-inch length makes it ideal for detail work on any cuisine—mincing herbs, slicing cooked meats against the grain, or dicing aromatics with precision. Where it struggles is rocking motions and large-format cutting. I regularly use mine for European-style prep, but I never attempt things like breaking down whole chickens or aggressive chopping. If you want a truly versatile all-purpose blade, a 8-inch chef's knife is the better choice. The Shun Premier excels as a specialist that occasionally covers general tasks, not the reverse.
