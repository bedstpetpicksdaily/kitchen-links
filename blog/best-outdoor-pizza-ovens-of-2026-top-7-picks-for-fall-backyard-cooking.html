What to Look For in a Fall Pizza Oven

Cooler weather changes the equation slightly. Look for ovens with good insulation, because ambient temperatures in the 40s and 50s will pull heat out of a thin-walled chamber faster than a July evening will. Preheat time matters more too — nobody wants to stand outside for 45 minutes waiting for stone temperature. Check that your oven's baking stone is at least 0.4 inches thick, and prioritize models with a built-in thermometer or an easy spot to point an infrared gun. Fuel access is the other consideration: propane is far more convenient in cold, damp fall conditions than trying to keep a wood fire going in the wind.

Getting the Most From Your Oven This Season

Preheat longer than you think you need to — an extra five minutes of stone soak time is the difference between a soggy base and a crackling crust. Keep your dough balls at room temperature for at least an hour before stretching, especially in cool weather, or they'll fight you on the peel. Turn your pizza every 20 seconds using a turning peel to prevent the side facing the flame from charring. And don't stop at pizza: high-heat ovens are outstanding for flatbreads, roasted vegetables, seared steaks, and even cast-iron desserts. Pair your Big Horn oven with one of their griddles or camping stoves and you've got a genuinely complete outdoor kitchen for under what a single premium oven usually costs.

Frequently Asked Questions

How hot does an outdoor pizza oven need to get for real Neapolitan pizza?

You want a stone temperature of roughly 750–900°F. Big Horn Outdoors ovens comfortably exceed 900°F, which cooks a 12-inch pie in about 60 to 90 seconds with proper leopard-spotting on the crust.

Can I use an outdoor pizza oven in cold or rainy fall weather?

Yes. Cooler temperatures simply mean a slightly longer preheat. Keep the oven under a covered patio or awning during rain, never operate it in enclosed spaces, and use a weatherproof cover for storage between cooks.

Is propane or wood better for a backyard pizza oven?

Propane offers faster, more consistent heat and easier temperature control, making it ideal for weeknights. Wood adds authentic smoke flavor but takes more attention. Big Horn's multi-fuel models let you choose based on the occasion.
