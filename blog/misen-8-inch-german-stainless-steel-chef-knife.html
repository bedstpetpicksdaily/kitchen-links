Frequently Asked Questions

How does the Misen compare to Victorinox or Mercer chef knives at similar prices?

Victorinox Fibrox (around $50) is lighter, easier to sharpen, and honestly more ergonomic for extended use, though it's considered 'generic' rather than premium. Mercer (around $70) offers similar German steel quality to Misen. The real difference is handle comfort and personal preference—all three are solid workhorses. If you have smaller hands, Victorinox wins. If you want something that feels slightly more premium in hand, Misen edges ahead.

Does this knife come sharp, or do I need to sharpen it before using?

Most reviewers report it arrives reasonably sharp and ready to use immediately. However, 'reasonably sharp' isn't the same as 'razor sharp'—you'll likely want to hit it with a honing steel or whetstone after your first week of use. German stainless blades ship at a thicker edge angle (around 20 degrees) compared to Japanese knives, so they're more durable but less sharp out of the box.

How often will I need to sharpen this, and is it difficult?

Expect to hone weekly with a steel (takes 30 seconds) and sharpen every 6-12 months depending on usage volume. Stainless steel is more forgiving than carbon steel—you won't ruin it by washing it in the dishwasher, though you shouldn't. For sharpening, a basic pull-through sharpener works, but a whetstone gives better results. September is actually a great time to invest in a good honing steel alongside this knife since people often overlook the maintenance component.

Is the 8-inch size right for me, or should I go smaller/larger?

8 inches is the standard professional size and works for 95% of home cooks. Go smaller (6-inch) only if you have very small hands or a cramped kitchen. Go larger (10-inch) only if you're regularly breaking down whole chickens or working with large vegetables—it becomes unwieldy for detail work. Stick with 8 inches unless you have a specific reason otherwise.

Can I put this in the dishwasher?

Technically you can, but don't. Stainless steel won't rust from it, but dishwasher detergent is harsh, and tumbling around with other utensils dulls the edge faster. Hand wash with warm soapy water, dry immediately, and store blade-up. This takes two minutes and extends the life of your knife by years.
