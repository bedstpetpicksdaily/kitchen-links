Pros & Cons

Pros
  • ✓ The air fryer function genuinely outperforms standalone air fryers in the $150-250 range. I tested chicken thighs, frozen fries, and salmon fillets—the SmartLid produced crispier exteriors and more even browning than my previous budget model. The larger cooking basket compared to compact air fryers means you can actually cook for a family without three separate batches.
  • ✓ Pressure cooking is fast and reliable. A bone broth that typically takes 4 hours on the stovetop finished in 45 minutes with noticeably better flavor concentration. The sealing mechanism feels solid, and the stainless steel construction won't degrade like cheaper plastic-heavy models.
  • ✓ The SmartLid design actually works. Being able to swap between lid types (pressure lid, air fryer lid, baking lid) without storing bulky attachments separately is genuinely convenient. I found myself using this flexibility daily—pressure cooking Monday's pot roast, air frying Wednesday's appetizers, baking a casserole Thursday.
  • ✓ Preset programs reduce guesswork. The guided cooking for common dishes like ribs, roasted vegetables, and steamed dumplings removes the trial-and-error phase. New users won't feel intimidated, and experienced cooks can override presets for customization.
Cons
  • ✗ The learning curve is real despite 'smart' branding. The control panel has substantial functionality, and figuring out which buttons control which features took me two weeks of regular use. The manual is comprehensive but dense—this isn't a plug-and-play appliance.
  • ✗ Capacity feels smaller once you account for pressure cooking requirements. You need headspace for steam release, which means actual usable volume is about 20% less than the advertised capacity. A family of five might find meal-prepping challenging without splitting recipes.

Our Verdict

The Ninja Foodi 11-in-1 SmartLid SmartLid deserves its solid 4.3-star rating—it's legitimately useful if you have the counter space and are willing to learn its systems. At current pricing, you're paying for versatility and build quality, not gimmicks. This makes sense if you're consolidating three separate appliances (pressure cooker, air fryer, oven) and actually use all three functions regularly. Skip it if you're hoping it'll replace a full-size oven or if you've already committed to a single-purpose air fryer or Instant Pot you love. For fall meal prep season, when you're likely managing everything from roasted vegetables to batch cooking proteins, the flexibility here pays dividends.

Frequently Asked Questions

Is the Ninja Foodi 11-in-1 better than buying separate air fryer and pressure cooker?

That depends on counter space and budget. Buying separate appliances ($200 air fryer + $150 pressure cooker) costs less upfront, but the Foodi saves roughly 1.5 feet of counter width and eliminates storage headaches. If your kitchen is cramped or you cook frequently, the integration justifies the price difference. If you have abundant counter space and buy budget models, separate appliances might be wiser.

How long does pressure cooking actually take compared to stovetop?

Dramatically faster. Tough cuts like beef chuck that need 90 minutes on the stovetop finish in 35-40 minutes under pressure. The tradeoff: preheating and pressure release add 10-15 minutes total. For meal prep and weeknight dinners, you're saving roughly 45-60 minutes per week in active cooking time.

Does the air fryer function compare to dedicated air fryer ovens?

For most home cooking, yes. The heating element and basket size produce results nearly identical to standalone air fryer ovens priced $300-400. The main limitation is batch size—you can't crisp 20 wings simultaneously like in a dedicated oven model. For everyday family meals, it performs exceptionally well; for entertaining crowds, a dedicated air fryer oven might edge ahead.
