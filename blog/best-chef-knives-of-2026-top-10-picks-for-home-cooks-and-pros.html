Frequently Asked Questions

What size chef knife should I buy?

An 8-inch chef knife is the sweet spot for most people — long enough to handle large produce and proteins, but manageable on a standard cutting board. Choose a 6-inch blade if you have smaller hands or a compact kitchen, or a 10-inch if you regularly break down large cuts of meat and want extra blade length.

Is a nakiri knife worth buying if I already own a chef knife?

If you prep a lot of vegetables, absolutely. A nakiri's flat profile makes full contact with the board on every cut, so you get clean, complete slices with a straight push-down motion instead of rocking. It's faster and less tiring than a chef knife for cabbage, carrots, squash, and herbs.

How do I care for a Damascus steel knife?

Hand wash with warm water and mild soap immediately after use, then dry thoroughly — never leave it in the sink or run it through a dishwasher. Use a wooden or plastic cutting board (never glass or stone), hone the edge every few weeks with a ceramic rod, and store it on a knife stand or magnetic strip rather than loose in a drawer.

Are knife sets better value than buying knives individually?

Usually yes, if the set contains blades you'll actually use. Butterfork's sets bundle the core essentials — chef, bread, nakiri, and paring knives — at a lower per-knife cost than buying separately, and many include a matching stand. If you only need one or two blades, buying individually avoids paying for pieces that sit unused.
